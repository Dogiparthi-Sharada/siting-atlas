================================================================================
  HOW TO FIND OPENING DATES FOR 13 DELIVERY STATIONS
  Free public records. No account, no payment. About 10 minutes each.
================================================================================

WHY THIS IS THE LAST BLOCKER

  Every facility we hold has an UPPER bound and no lower bound. OSHA proves
  the building was operating by some date; nothing tells us when it opened.
  That gap now blocks three separate things:

      the 13 facilities below cannot enter the model at all
      the warehousing covariate may contain its own outcome, because
        open_year is currently just the OSHA bound restated
      the line-haul covariate cannot be tested

  We tried satellite imagery. It failed -- 36% of its estimates were
  logically impossible. County records are the remaining free route, and
  unlike satellite they are a MEASUREMENT rather than an inference.


WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING FOR, IN PRIORITY ORDER

  1. CERTIFICATE OF OCCUPANCY date       the building may legally be used.
                                          Closest thing to an opening date.
  2. FINAL BUILDING INSPECTION date      usually within weeks of the above.
  3. BUILDING PERMIT ISSUED date         construction STARTED. A lower
                                          bound, still useful.
  4. ASSESSOR "YEAR BUILT"               year only, but every county has it
                                          and it takes two minutes.

  Any one of these is worth having. Number 4 alone would unblock the model.
  Do not spend long hunting for 1 if 4 is on the screen in front of you.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  THE FASTEST ROUTE -- TRY THIS FIRST FOR EVERY SITE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Search the county ASSESSOR, not the city. Assessors index by address and
  publish "year built" for every parcel, because it drives the tax bill.

  Search the web for:

      <county name> county assessor parcel search

  Then enter the street address. Look for a field called Year Built,
  Effective Year Built, or Actual Year Built.

  CAUTION. A warehouse is often built years before Amazon leases it. Year
  Built tells you when the BUILDING appeared, not when Amazon arrived. If
  Year Built is well before the OSHA date, note it and move to the permit
  search -- Amazon usually files a tenant-improvement permit when it takes
  a building over, and THAT is the date we want.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  IF THE ASSESSOR IS NOT ENOUGH -- THE PERMIT PORTAL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Most US cities run one of four systems. Search:

      <city name> building permit search

  You will land on Accela Citizen Access, eTRAKiT, CitizenServe or Tyler
  EnerGov. All four work the same way:

      1. Choose "Search Permits" or "Permit Search"
      2. Enter the street number and street name ONLY. Leave out the
         suffix -- "17341 Minnezona" finds more than
         "17341 West Minnezona Avenue"
      3. Widen the date range to cover 2015 to today
      4. Look for permit types: NEW COMMERCIAL, SHELL BUILDING, TENANT
         IMPROVEMENT, CERTIFICATE OF OCCUPANCY

  Amazon files under many names. If a search by address returns nothing,
  try the APPLICANT or OWNER field with:

      Amazon        Amazon.com Services       Amazon Logistics
      Amazon.com    Amazon.com Services LLC   Amazon Fulfillment Services

  and the landlord, since Amazon usually leases:

      Prologis      Duke Realty     First Industrial     STAG Industrial


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  WHAT TO WRITE DOWN
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Fill in dates_found.csv -- a template is in this folder with the 13 rows
  already there. Columns:

      facility_id        leave as is
      address            leave as is
      found_date         YYYY-MM-DD, or YYYY if you only have a year
      date_type          co / final_inspection / permit_issued / year_built
      source_url         the page you found it on
      source_name        e.g. "Maricopa County Assessor"
      confidence         high / medium / low -- your judgement
      notes              anything odd. Especially: if the building predates
                         Amazon, say so and give both dates if you have them

  A BLANK ROW IS A VALID ANSWER. If a county has nothing online, write
  "not found" in notes and move on. Nine of thirteen is a good result and
  far better than a guessed date -- a wrong date is worse than no date,
  because the model cannot tell it is wrong.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  IF YOU ONLY HAVE TIME FOR SOME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Do these five first. They are the ones most likely to have a clean online
  record and they span the widest range of years, which is what the model
  needs most:

      2   Phoenix, AZ        Maricopa is one of the best portals in the US
      9   Bethpage, NY       Nassau County publishes permits online
      10  Hampton, VA        small city, good records
      11  Virginia Beach, VA recent, so easy to find
      13  Greenville, WI     Outagamie County has a clean portal

  Five dates would be enough to test whether the OSHA lag is short or long,
  which is the question underneath all of this. If Amazon typically opens
  within months of the OSHA inspection, the current open_year is nearly
  right and defect 1 shrinks. If the lag is years, the warehousing covariate
  has a real problem and we need to say so.
