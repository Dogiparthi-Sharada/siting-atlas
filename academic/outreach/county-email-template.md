# Outreach to county planning departments

**Purpose: validation, not data.** You are asking whether the tool answers a
question they actually had. You are *not* asking them to send you parcel files
— that is weeks of chasing and the evidence says it would not rescue the
model. Four replies saying "we needed X and could not get it" is worth more to
the grant than another dataset.

Keep it under 200 words. Planners get a lot of student email and most of it
asks them to do unpaid work.

---

## Who to write to

Target counties that have **hosted an Amazon delivery station since 2018** —
you have 195 metros with a dated opening, so you can name the facility in the
email, which is what stops it looking like a mass mailing.

Best recipients, in order:

1. **Planning Director** or **Community Development Director** — they ran the
   hearing
2. **Economic Development** — they negotiated the incentive
3. **County GIS Coordinator** — usually the most responsive, and often knows
   who else to ask

Find them on the county's own site. Avoid general info@ addresses.

---

## The email

> **Subject:** Research question from CSU East Bay — the Amazon facility at
> [STREET, CITY]
>
> Dear [NAME],
>
> I am a graduate student at California State University, East Bay, working on
> a funded research project about how much communities can learn about
> warehouse siting decisions using only public data.
>
> Your county approved an Amazon delivery station at [ADDRESS] in [MONTH
> YEAR]. I am not asking for any records — I am trying to understand what
> information your team wished it had **at the time**.
>
> Three short questions, and a one-line answer to any of them would help:
>
> 1. When the application came in, what did you most want to know that you
>    could not find out?
> 2. Did you have any way to estimate what the site was worth to the
>    applicant?
> 3. Did you know how the incentives being discussed compared with what other
>    counties had given?
>
> I have built a free tool that estimates the delivery-cost advantage of a
> specific parcel to an operator, and I would value five minutes of your
> reaction to whether that is useful or beside the point.
>
> Happy to share the results with you either way.
>
> With thanks,
> [NAME]
> [PROGRAMME], California State University, East Bay
> [EMAIL]

---

## Why it is written this way

**"I am not asking for any records"** in the second paragraph. This is the
sentence that gets it read. Most academic email to a planning department is a
records request in disguise, and they are trained to route those to a clerk.

**It names their facility with a date.** You can do this because you extracted
1,420 opening dates. It proves you did the work before writing, and it is the
single strongest signal that this is not a form letter.

**The three questions are answerable in one line each** and are about their
experience, not their files. A planner can reply from a phone.

**"beside the point" is deliberate.** You are inviting them to tell you the
tool is not useful. That makes the reply honest, and a reply saying *"what we
actually needed was traffic projections"* is a finding you can quote.

**"Happy to share the results with you either way"** — reciprocity, and it
costs nothing.

---

## What to do with the replies

Log every one, including non-replies, in a simple table: county, facility,
role, date sent, replied yes/no, and the verbatim answer. **The non-response
rate is itself a measurement** and should be reported — if 3 of 30 planning
departments respond to a question about a facility in their own jurisdiction,
that is evidence about institutional capacity, not just about your email.

Two quotable outcomes, both useful:

- *"We had no way to value the site"* → confirms the tool addresses a real gap
- *"We needed something else entirely"* → redirects the tool, and is a better
  finding than confirmation

---

## Practical notes

- **Send 20-30, not 5.** Expect a low reply rate; planners are busy and this
  is unsolicited.
- **Check whether CSUEB requires IRB review.** Asking professionals about
  their job in a professional capacity is usually exempt, but "usually" is not
  "certainly", and the answer takes one email to your research office. Do this
  **before** sending.
- **Send from your university address.** A `.edu` sender clears filters that a
  personal account does not.
- **Do not attach anything.** Attachments from unknown senders get quarantined.
- **One follow-up after two weeks, then stop.** More than that is pestering.
