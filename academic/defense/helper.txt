--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  0.  THE SIX RULES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  RULE 0   SAY "IT FAILED" IN THOSE WORDS - AND SAY IT TWICE.
           There are TWO siting models now and BOTH concessions are
           yours to make before anyone makes them for you.
           ONE. The discrete-time hazard model over ZIP-quarters was
           built, fitted on real data, and it failed. It is RETIRED.
           TWO. Its successor, a conditional ZCTA choice model, IS
           fitted - on the national frame, 94 decisions - and on the
           38 held-out decisions it is BEATEN by a single raw Census
           warehousing count with nothing fitted from it: 8 top-1
           against 7, 20 top-10 against 19. Say "beaten". Never
           "matched". The estimation buys nothing measurable.
           Every softer phrasing - refined, evolved, under-powered,
           directionally right - costs you credibility you then have to
           spend defending the components that DO work. Lead with the
           failure and the rest of the viva is about the project rather
           than about whether you can be trusted to describe it.

  RULE 1   NAME THE WEAKNESS BEFORE THEY DO.
           Every limitation you volunteer with its mitigation and its
           residual buys credibility for the claims you keep. Every one you
           defend past the point of defensibility spends it.

  RULE 2   NEVER SAY "FIRST" OR "NOVEL".
           Say "not published in the open literature" or "not available as
           something anyone can check". Those stay true whatever any vendor
           ships and whatever the operator runs internally.

  RULE 3   IF YOU DON'T KNOW, SAY SO AND SAY WHAT YOU'D DO.
           "We can't measure that - here's what we report instead" is a
           complete answer. Bluffing is the only unrecoverable failure.

  RULE 4   ANSWER THE QUESTION THEY ASKED.
           Not the adjacent one you prepared. If you need a moment, repeat
           the question back in your own words. That buys four seconds and
           checks you understood it.

  RULE 5   NEVER QUOTE A NUMBER THIS PROJECT HAS NOT COMPUTED.
           The defence pack contains illustrative arithmetic - an 8 km
           decay band, a rank of 1 in 40, a first-stage F, a PR-AUC, a
           precision@100. NONE of those is a measurement. If you are asked
           for one, the answer is "that is the design, I have not run it".
           A negative result you can defend is survivable. An invented
           figure is not - it turns a negative result into an integrity
           problem, and there is no recovering from that in the room.
           ONE THING MOVED OFF THIS LIST. The Monte Carlo RAN - 500 of
           500 draws, seed 20260914, montecarlo_report.json. Its bands
           ARE quotable and are in Sec. 5. The rank-stability example
           (87% / 34%) is still made up and stays on the list.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  1.  THE ANSWER SHAPE  -  use this every time
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

      1. NAME IT          "The ACS covariates lag 18 to 24 months."
      2. SIZE IT          "That affects level features, not rate-of-change
                           features."
      3. WORK AROUND      "We blend monthly rents and permits, and align
                           covariate vintage to decision vintage - the
                           operator faced the same lag."
      4. STATE RESIDUAL   "An area that changed in the last 18 months with
                           no permit signal will be misclassified. We flag
                           those low-confidence."

  Never stop at 1. Never skip 4. Step 4 is what makes steps 2 and 3 believable.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  2.  THE EIGHT HARDEST QUESTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Ranked by how badly a weak answer hurts you.


  ============================================================================
  HARD #1   "Why did the method change again?"
  ============================================================================

  WHY IT'S HARD   Any answer that sounds like taste rather than evidence
                  reads as indecision, and indecision is the impression that
                  costs you the room.

  DO NOT          Say "we refined the approach" or "the data suggested a
                  different direction". Both are true and both sound like
                  you are hiding a failure.

  SAY FIRST       "I specified a model, built it, tested it, and it failed.
                   Then I went to the literature to find out why."

  THEN            "The hazard model over ZIP-quarters was built and fitted
                   on the 43-building panel. It lost to a model that
                   predicts the same constant everywhere: AUC 0.689, Brier
                   skill half a percent, calibration 170x worse than the
                   constant, negative skill on both hold-outs.
                   So I went to Train, Discrete Choice Methods. The
                   assumption I broke is in Section 3.7.1, printed page 61:
                   the likelihood is a PRODUCT over decision makers, and
                   that product is only a likelihood if each decision maker's
                   choice is INDEPENDENT of every other's. Mine were not. A
                   station switches on every ZIP within 15 miles at once -
                   median 58, mean 88, largest 307. So 812 ZIP-quarter
                   events were 43 real decisions wearing geometry as a
                   disguise. The standard name is CLUSTERING, or
                   pseudo-replication.
                   Same question, correct unit. Train Section 2.2 is the
                   authority for building the SUCCESSOR choice set - which
                   ZIP, conditional on a station opening in metro m in
                   period t - and that successor is now fitted."

  THE CITATION    An earlier version of this sheet blamed Train Section 2.2
  CORRECTION      and MUTUAL EXCLUSIVITY. That was the wrong section, and
                  volunteering the correction makes the argument STRONGER,
                  so say it if the examiner has read Train.
                  Section 2.2 governs a choice set facing a DECISION MAKER.
                  A hazard on area-quarters has no decision maker choosing
                  among those rows, so exclusivity is not a property they
                  can have or lack. Worse for the old rhetoric, Train calls
                  that criterion "not restrictive" and says "Appropriate
                  definition of alternatives can nearly always assure that
                  the alternatives are mutually exclusive" (p. 12), and
                  gives a two-line repair. If the examiner knows the book,
                  the old citation hands them the win. The independence
                  assumption has no two-line repair, which is exactly why
                  the corrected diagnosis is the stronger one.

  IF PUSHED       "So it was wrong from the start?"
                  -> "It was UNTESTED from the start, which is worse in one
                      way and better in another. Worse, because it went into
                      a proposal untested. Better, because a specification
                      that was tested, failed, and was replaced for an
                      articulable reason is worth more than one that was
                      never stress-tested. The ZIP-quarter unit came in at
                      proposal v4 and was never checked against Train's rule."

  WHY THIS WORKS  It converts "you keep changing your mind" into "I ran the
                  test that most projects skip". Own the provenance of the
                  mistake and it stops being a wound.


  ============================================================================
  HARD #2   "Your model doesn't work. Why should I pass this?"
  ============================================================================

  WHY IT'S HARD   It is the hardest question in the viva and it is fair.
                  Flinching loses more than the failure does.

  DO NOT          Soften it. Do not say "under-powered" or "directionally
                  right". Do not oversell the four components that work.

  SAY             "You are right about both of them, and I'd rather say it
                   in those words. Four reasons it is still worth passing.
                   ONE, both negative results are MEASURED, not vague.
                   The hazard model: AUC 0.689 against a null of 0.500,
                   Brier skill +0.0047, calibration 170x the null's,
                   negative skill on both hold-outs - hazard_report.json.
                   Its successor, the fitted choice model: on 38 held-out
                   decisions it gets 7 top-1 and 19 top-10, and a single
                   raw Census warehousing count with NOTHING fitted from it
                   gets 8 and 20. The estimation bought nothing I can
                   measure - choice_report.json, and you can regenerate it.
                   TWO, both come with a DIAGNOSIS. The hazard model broke
                   INDEPENDENCE ACROSS OBSERVATIONS - Train 3.7.1, p. 61 -
                   because a 15-mile catchment ties a median of 58 ZIPs to
                   one building. The choice model is not broken; it is
                   simply not better than its own best single covariate,
                   and with 56 training decisions and three parameters,
                   two of them pinned at the boundary, that is the result
                   the sample supports.
                   THREE, 'the operator builds where warehouses already
                   are' is a real, useful finding - twice as good as a
                   population map at top-10, 20 against 10 - and it needs
                   no model at all. Reporting that a raw count beats my
                   estimator is the finding, not an admission.
                   FOUR, the other components work and never needed the
                   facility panel: cost model over 2,333 ZIPs, portfolio
                   optimiser, the 33,791-ZCTA warehouse, six agent gates,
                   638 tests passing and 2 xfailed."

  IF PUSHED       "Isn't that rationalisation after the fact?"
                  -> "It would be if the diagnosis arrived with the excuse.
                      The power check that reports 7.6 events per parameter
                      runs every time the model runs and was written BEFORE
                      the result - it reports three different event counts
                      precisely so the flattering one cannot be the one
                      quoted. The 4-to-345-month date measurement was made
                      before the model was fitted. The machinery that says
                      this model is weak was built into the model."

  IF PUSHED       "Why not just get more data?"
                  -> "Five routes failed, each documented with the artefact
                      that proves it. The sixth gave 43 buildings. MWPVL
                      sells the panel Houde et al. used, and buying it would
                      dissolve the problem and the point of the project at
                      the same time - which is that a city should be able to
                      do this from free federal records. That decision is
                      written down in METHODS_RESEARCH Sec. 9."

  NEVER SAY       That the failure was the plan. It was not.


  ============================================================================
  HARD #3   "Did you just copy a paper?"
  ============================================================================

  WHY IT'S HARD   Two Econometrica papers do look like this project from a
                  distance. If you answer vaguely you sound derivative.

  WHY IT'S EASY   This is the STRONGEST card in the pack, because the answer
                  is evidence rather than assertion.

  SAY             "Neither paper studies the facility I study, and neither
                   could be reproduced without a purchase order.
                   HOLMES 2011, Econometrica 79(1), on Wal-Mart. His Sec. 3:
                   the store data 'was obtained from Trade Dimensions, a unit
                   of ACNielsen'. Purchased.
                   HOUDE, NEWBERRY & SEIM 2023, Econometrica 91(1) 147-190,
                   on Amazon. Their Sec. 2.2: they obtain the network 'from
                   the supply-chain consulting company MWPVL, International'.
                   Also purchased.
                   And the clincher: their Sec. 1 says they focus on
                   fulfillment centers and sortation centers, and they drop
                   specialised centres including PrimeNow Hubs. I searched
                   their published text for 'delivery station'. ZERO HITS.
                   So: facility type, delivery stations, unstudied in either.
                   Data provenance, 5.2m free federal OSHA records against
                   two purchased panels. Question, who gets served and can a
                   city check it, against nexus tax policy.
                   I do NOT claim a new estimator. Both papers use the same
                   machinery and if I go that route I'll be using theirs."

  IF PUSHED       "Is a new dataset really a contribution?"
                  -> "On its own, no. Here it is the only free route to this
                      facility class, and the route itself is a finding:
                      five methods failed before one worked, and the one that
                      worked measures something other than an opening date.
                      Both halves are written down."

  DO NOT SAY      "State grain." An earlier draft said Houde et al. worked at
                  state grain. Wrong. Demand is COUNTY grain, supply is a
                  20-mile cluster; state is the unit of the TAX variation.


  ============================================================================
  HARD #4   "Your instrument is invalid. The exclusion restriction fails."
  ============================================================================

  WHY IT'S HARD   It's correct. The restriction is not testable and ours is
                  contestable. Any attempt to argue it away loses.
                  It is ALSO not built, and that has to be said.

  DO NOT          Claim the restriction holds. Cite the F-statistic as if it
                  addressed exclusion (it addresses relevance). And do NOT
                  quote a first-stage F - no such number exists in any
                  artefact in this repository. Inventing one is the single
                  unrecoverable error available to you in this viva.

  SAY             "Two answers, and the second matters more.
                   First: it is contestable and I'd rather say so myself.
                   Commercial zoning correlates with employment density and
                   traffic, either of which could enter residential demand
                   directly.
                   Second: it is not instrumenting anything yet. The design
                   reports a first-stage F for relevance and a sensitivity
                   path for exclusion. Neither has been run, because both sit
                   behind a siting model that does not work. Correcting
                   selection in a specification whose unit of analysis is
                   wrong is polishing the wrong object."

  THE ORDER       Fix the unit of analysis FIRST - clustered observations
                  are not a selection problem and no selection correction
                  repairs them. Then ask about selection. The other order
                  gives you a corrected estimate of something that was
                  never a choice probability.

  BACKUP          The Heckman two-step is designed, not built. Say
                  "designed, not built". Do not say "run on one metro".


  ============================================================================
  HARD #5   "You can't observe revenue, so the NPV is meaningless."
  ============================================================================

  WHY IT'S HARD   Half true. The dollar figure IS unidentified.

  DO NOT          Defend a point estimate of NPV. Invent a margin.

  SAY             "The dollar figure is unidentified and we make no accuracy
                   claim on it. But six of our eight outputs don't need it:
                   ranking is invariant to a common positive scale factor, so
                   the ranked list, the bundle choice, rank stability,
                   break-even thresholds, timing and the selection propensity
                   are all unaffected.
                   For the two that do need it, we factor margin out and
                   report NPV as a_i times m minus b_i, with the break-even
                   value. A reader with a view on margin substitutes theirs.
                   And we validate the half we can observe - capital against
                   disclosed capex, target within 25 percent."

  THE COUNTER     "But if margin varies by area, ranking moves too."
                  -> "Correct, and that's the one case that breaks
                      scale-invariance. We re-rank under a margin varying
                      with income as a worst case and report top-100 churn."
                  Concede this fast. It is a good objection and conceding it
                  makes the rest credible.


  ============================================================================
  HARD #6   "Isn't this just a worse version of what Esri already sells?"
  ============================================================================

  WHY IT'S HARD   Partly true on capability. Sounds dismissive.

  DO NOT          Get defensive. Overstate the technical difference.

  SAY             "On trade-area geometry, yes - theirs is better and I'd
                   use it. But their cannibalization tool measures trade-area
                   OVERLAP, which is descriptive geometry. It answers 'how
                   much do these areas share'. It does not answer 'what would
                   volume in B have been if A had never opened'. That is the
                   distinction that survives - it is a distinction between
                   two DESIGNS, and I have to be straight that ours is
                   designed and not yet built. What runs in our optimiser
                   today is a 20 km / 0.18 assumed penalty, which is the same
                   KIND of object as theirs. The causal version is the second
                   estimand and it is untouched.
                   And structurally: every commercial tool models your OWN
                   network from licensed data for a client who paid. None
                   forecasts a competitor's network from public data, and
                   none can be opened by a city council. That part is true
                   today, not in the design."

  THE PIVOT       "Every technique in this project has a paywalled
                   equivalent. What doesn't exist is a version anyone can
                   check."


  ============================================================================
  HARD #7   "What is the weakest part of this project?"
  ============================================================================

  WHY IT'S HARD   Any deflection reads as evasion. "Nothing" is fatal.

  DO NOT          Name something trivial. Name something you've already fixed.

  SAY             "The siting model, and it isn't close. Both versions.
                   The retired hazard model was fitted on the real panel and
                   does not work: AUC 0.689, Brier skill half a percent,
                   calibration 170x worse than a constant, negative skill on
                   both hold-outs.
                   Its successor IS fitted, and it is BEATEN on held-out
                   data by a raw Census warehousing count with nothing
                   fitted from it - 8 top-1 against my 7, 20 top-10 against
                   my 19. Three free parameters and two of them sit at the
                   boundary at 3e-16 and 5e-16, so one parameter is doing
                   all the work. The estimation buys nothing measurable.
                   I lead with that rather than being walked to it.
                   Root cause: a target that cannot carry a siting model -
                   43 buildings on the pilot frame, 94 decisions nationally,
                   56 of them used for training, and most dates are OSHA
                   'operating by' upper bounds.
                   SECOND weakest, and worth naming so it doesn't look like
                   I only admit the obvious one: the intervals landed on
                   the last day and they COVER THE NULL. The null here is
                   beta = 1, not beta = 0 - households is the numeraire, so
                   1 means 'worth the same as one household'. Sandwich 95%
                   on the only interior parameter is [0.734, 2.835],
                   z = 1.064, p = 0.287. Bootstrap over decisions
                   [0.760, 4.762], over metros [0.702, 10.013], BCa
                   [0.714, 3.522]. All four cover 1.0. So the one covariate
                   that does any work is not distinguishable from one more
                   household. The sandwich is refused at the two boundary
                   parameters and that refusal is right: its asymptotics
                   assume an interior maximum with a vanishing gradient.
                   THIRD: the cannibalisation parameter in the optimiser is
                   ASSUMED, not estimated - 20 km radius, a linear distance
                   taper, a 0.18 peak. No standard error, no donor pool, no
                   weights matrix W anywhere in the source tree. It feeds
                   NPV directly, which is why we report a break-even margin
                   and a 500-draw Monte Carlo band rather than point NPVs."

  WHY THIS WORKS  It names the real thing first, sizes it with measurements,
                  and then volunteers a SECOND and THIRD weakness
                  unprompted. Naming more than one is what separates "I know
                  where my inference is load-bearing" from "I rehearsed one
                  admission".

  DO NOT SAY      That the intervals rescue the model. They do the
                  opposite - they agree with the ranking defeat. And do not
                  say "the interval excludes zero": it does, and that is
                  meaningless here, because the null is 1. Quoting the zero
                  test would be technically true and completely misleading.
                  Say the interval COVERS THE NULL.
                  One more: the inference code landed on 2026-09-14 and was
                  uncommitted. If asked when, say so plainly.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  3.  TRAPS  -  questions designed to make you overclaim
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  TRAP   "So you can predict where Amazon will build next?"
  RISK   Saying yes without qualification.
  SAY    "We predict what their revealed preference implies for areas they
          haven't reached. That's operator-consistent desirability, not
          objective viability - the distinction matters because the model
          learns their historical pattern, including any part of it we
          wouldn't endorse."

  TRAP   "Is this publishable?"
  RISK   Overselling to a room that knows what publishable means.
  SAY    "One component plausibly is - the inferential-integrity gates, at
          an AI-safety workshop. Beyond that, the honest candidates are the
          data-collection record and the negative result: '43 delivery
          stations dated by federal inspection records cannot identify a
          siting policy' is a measured statement about what public data can
          do for a facility class nobody has studied. That is a short paper,
          not a big one. I would NOT claim an estimated decay parameter -
          that was designed and never built."

  TRAP   "How accurate is it?"
  RISK   Giving one number for a system with four different outputs.
  SAY    "Depends which output, and we're explicit about that." Then split:
          cost to serve  -> engineering model, no statistical accuracy claim.
                            Sensitivity of every parameter is published.
          hazard model   -> RETIRED, and it FAILED. Say so first, then the
                            numbers: AUC 0.689 against a NULL MODEL's 0.500,
                            and worse calibrated than a constant. (0.500 is
                            the null's AUC, not the base rate - the base rate
                            is 2.0%. Do not mix those up in the room.)
                            Do not lead with AUC; AUC is monotone-invariant
                            so it cannot see the calibration failure that
                            sank the model.
          choice model   -> fitted, and BEATEN by one raw covariate. On 38
                            held-out decisions: 7 top-1, 16 top-5, 19 top-10.
                            Raw warehousing count: 8, 16, 20. Give BOTH rows
                            or you have oversold it. In-sample McFadden
                            rho-squared 0.197 on the 56 training decisions -
                            quote it as in-sample or not at all.
          dollar NPV     -> no accuracy claim; reported as a break-even
                            margin (1.3431) because the operator's margin is
                            unobservable, plus a 500-draw Monte Carlo band.

  TRAP   "You changed your headline metric after the model failed."
  RISK   Defending the sequence, or claiming AUC was unfair. It was not
         unfair - it was the FLATTERING metric. And the sequence really was
         failure-then-change, so denying it loses the room.
  SAY    "Partly fair, and I'll give you the part that lands first. I
          pre-registered AUC, measured 0.6894, and THEN read the scoring-
          rules literature. Wrong order - I should have registered a proper
          score at design time. What I'll defend is the destination.
          AUC isn't a scoring rule at all: propriety means your expected
          score is maximised by quoting what you actually believe, and it's
          defined on one forecast and one observation. AUC is a rank
          statistic over PAIRS, so the guarantee isn't available for it.
          Gneiting and Raftery, JASA 2007 - nineteen years before my
          result. Section 8 of that paper shows two improper scores picking
          the wrong weather model on sixteen thousand real forecasts."
  NEVER  "Gneiting and Raftery show AUC is improper." THEY NEVER MENTION
         AUC. Zero occurrences in twenty pages. The argument is ours, the
         framework is theirs, and the fabricated version is checkable in
         thirty seconds.
  ALSO   Do not say "Brier skill is the proper scoring rule". The BRIER
         SCORE is strictly proper; the SKILL score is generally improper -
         same paper, page 362. Report the raw pair: 0.019522 model against
         0.019614 null, same 8,044 rows. Skill is how you display it.
  FULL   VIVA_QA.md D5.

  TRAP   "Did AI write this?"
  RISK   Sounding defensive.
  SAY    "Yes - for code and literature triage, and it's also a component:
          an agent writes to the analytical model behind gates. The
          interesting part was finding where it breaks. Standard gates protect
          data integrity; none check whether a valid write invalidates a
          causal assumption. That's the failure class we found."

  TRAP   "Why should a regulator trust a student project?"
  RISK   Defending your credentials instead of the artifact.
  SAY    "They shouldn't trust it - they should check it. That's the point.
          Every input is public, the code is open, and the panel is
          published. It's the only model in this space they CAN check."


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  4.  IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  YOU DON'T KNOW THE ANSWER
      "I don't know. Here's how I'd find out: [method]. Can I follow up?"
      Then actually follow up. This costs you nothing.

  THEY FIND AN ERROR IN A SLIDE
      "You're right, that's wrong. [Correct it.] Does it change the
       conclusion? [Answer honestly.]"
      Do not argue. Do not explain how it happened.

  THEY CITE A PAPER YOU HAVEN'T READ
      "I haven't read that one. What does it establish?" Then engage with
      the substance they give you. Never pretend.
      Note: we found Houde/Newberry/Seim and Pollmann exactly this way -
      by checking rather than assuming. Say so if it helps.

  THEY DISAGREE WITH A DESIGN CHOICE
      "That's a fair alternative. We chose X because [reason]. The tradeoff
       is [tradeoff]. If you think [their option] is better, that changes
       [specific consequence] - is that what you'd want?"
      Turn it into a design conversation, not a defence.

  A TEAMMATE GIVES A WEAK ANSWER
      Do not correct them in the room unless it's factually wrong AND
      material. If it is: "Let me add to that - [correction]." Never
      "actually, no".

  YOU'RE RUNNING LONG
      Skip: slides 3, 21, 23, 24 in the overview deck. Never skip 6, 9, 14
      or 17.

  A QUESTION IS HOSTILE RATHER THAN CURIOUS
      Answer the technical content, ignore the tone, keep your pace. Do not
      match energy. The room notices who stayed calm.
