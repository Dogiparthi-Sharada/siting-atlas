===============================================================================
  WHY THIS MATTERS (read once, then skip)
===============================================================================

The model asks: "which ZIP code did the operator switch on, and when?"

To answer that I need a list of delivery facilities with OPENING DATES. From
that I compute, for every ZIP and every quarter, whether it was being served.
That column is called `enabled`. It was 0% populated when this was written;
it is now 2.69% of 1,081,312 national cells, and 51.0% of the 2,413 ZCTAs
in the ten pilot metros are enabled at some point.

A facility you MISS is worse than one you get slightly wrong. A missing
facility becomes a ZIP that looks unserved when it was served - a false zero.
False zeros do not add noise, they add bias, all in one direction. So
COMPLETENESS beats volume every time.

       +---------------------------------------------------------+
       |  RULE:  a complete list of ONE operator in ONE metro     |
       |         beats a patchy list of four operators everywhere |
       +---------------------------------------------------------+

  TASK 0  -  UNDERSTAND THE TWO TRAPS  (5 minutes, do not skip)

TRAP 1 - THE AIRPORT CODE LIE
-----------------------------
Amazon names buildings after the nearest big AIRPORT, not the city they are
in. This already cost us 4 of the 8 rows you sent.

    OAK3  is in Patterson, CA     -> Stanislaus County, 70 mi from Oakland
    OAK4  is in Tracy, CA         -> San Joaquin County, NOT the Bay Area
    SCK3  is in Manteca, CA       -> San Joaquin County
    SMF5  is in Vacaville, CA     -> Solano County (Vallejo metro)

  NEVER decide the metro from the facility code.
  ALWAYS decide it from the ZIP CODE.

TRAP 2 - EXCEL EATS LEADING ZEROS
---------------------------------
    01890  becomes  1890  and every join silently drops that row.

  When you save the CSV, the `zip` column MUST be formatted as TEXT.
  In Excel:  select the column -> Format Cells -> Text -> re-enter values.
  In Google Sheets: Format -> Number -> Plain text BEFORE pasting.
  Safest: edit the CSV in a plain text editor, not a spreadsheet.

  My validator catches this, so you will not ship it silently - but it is
  faster to avoid than to fix.

COLUMN BY COLUMN
----------------
  facility_id    any unique string. AMZ-<code> is fine.        REQUIRED
  operator       Amazon                                        REQUIRED
  facility_type  one of: DS SDC SC FC AMXL                     REQUIRED
  city           city name                                     REQUIRED
  state          2-letter                                      REQUIRED
  zip            5 digits, TEXT FORMAT, leading zeros intact   REQUIRED
  latitude       decimal, e.g. 41.8781. blank is OK            optional
  longitude      decimal, NEGATIVE in the US, e.g. -87.6298    optional
  open_year      4 digits, e.g. 2021                           REQUIRED
  open_quarter   1 2 3 or 4.  SEE NOTE BELOW.                  IMPORTANT
  square_feet    integer, blank is OK                          optional
  status         open | closed | announced                     REQUIRED
  source_url     the page you got the DATE from                REQUIRED
  source_type    press_release | news | permit | company_page  REQUIRED
  confidence     high | medium | low                           REQUIRED

NOTE ON open_quarter - THIS ONE MATTERS
  My panel is QUARTERLY. 7 of your 8 rows had no quarter.
  If you know the month, convert:
        Jan Feb Mar = 1     Jul Aug Sep = 3
        Apr May Jun = 2     Oct Nov Dec = 4
  If you truly only know the year, leave it BLANK and set
  confidence = low. Do not guess a quarter - blank is honest,
  a guess is a fabricated event date and I will have to discard it.

NOTE ON confidence
  high    = official Amazon press release, or a permit with a date
  medium  = local news article stating the opening
  low     = year only, or inferred from a job posting / satellite imagery

NOTE ON status
  Include CLOSED facilities too, with their open date. A facility that
  opened in 2019 and closed in 2023 is a real event in both directions and
  the model needs both. Amazon closed a number of sites in 2022-23.

NOTE ON PRE-2018 FACILITIES
  Include them. My panel starts in 2018, so a facility that opened in 2014
  is not an "event" - but it tells me that ZIP was ALREADY being served, and
  without it the model thinks the ZIP was waiting to be switched on when it
  was not. These rows are cheap to collect and they matter.
